# Executive Summary

The MVP is a Rust protocol client and its supporting services. A single machine-level daemon serves npm's registry protocol to package managers from whichever of a plaintext cache, a peer swarm, and the upstream registry delivers within the configured source deadline, never slower than the registry alone by more than the hedge delay, while requesting entitlements and prefetching ciphertext in the background so a first run leaves the machine independent; authorizes every decryption attempt from a fresh view of Base state; decrypts locally with a native credential delivered inside settlement; and seeds ciphertext it cannot read. A contract suite on Base, identified by its virtual machine and its source language, the EVM suite in Solidity as the on-chain concrete of the chain family, holds canonical identities, deployments, parameter sets, envelope keys, entitlements with interval state, escrow, claims, and a delivery verifier bound through its adapter registry, over the BLS12-381 and BN254 precompiles. Every external touchpoint is an adapter family: a factory crate that owns the generic interface and its capability declaration, with each implementation a private concrete beneath it, so a consumer names the factory and never an implementation. A relayer sponsors the free path, a claim verifier attests maintainers, and a project seed host and site carry the core closure and the education tier. Two onboarding shells, a desktop application, and a CLI attach to the daemon over authenticated IPC. A validation harness is built first and its delivery verifier is the shipped contract.

What must function, in one sentence per family: install on Windows, macOS, and Linux through either shell to one identical postcondition after only permitted consent; serve packages from sources tried in order and hedged, never slower than the registry alone by more than the hedge delay, with a verified atomic CAS, and leave a first run independent of the registry asset by asset through batched grant requests and background prefetch; authenticate every descriptor before any credential is exercised and authorize every attempt from a multi-node state view; bootstrap absent packages in the background without blocking the initiating install and win or lose the registration race cleanly; move ciphertext by root across transports with non-authoritative discovery and a persistent seed host; deliver credentials at mint, grant, and sale with on-chain proof verification and atomic payment; hold identity under a custody adapter that keeps the holder seed on root devices, admits every other device under its own key as a signer on the identity's contract account, declares device roles, and upgrades in place; implement the cryptography exactly and measure its cost against the configured defaults; publish explicitly with dependency-closure ingestion and let maintainers claim escrowed records without the First Finder; sponsor free onboarding, batched requests, and fulfilling grants under a global budget; and observe every install without recording a secret. Completion is every acceptance scenario through packaged applications on clean machines.

#### Chain family ownership

- The chain forms family's factory owns `IChainForms`, the identity, entitlement, interval, and chain-identifier forms and the signed-intent form as associated types, each bounded by the encoding family's canonical-field contract, the entitlement contract taking the identity form, the forms identifier, and the declaration and mock; the family-owned functions are the declaration lookup `chain-forms/lookup_chain_forms_declaration`, the selection admission `chain-forms/admit_chain_forms_selection`, the composition admission `chain-forms/admit_chain_forms_composition`, and `chain-forms/factory`.
- `chain-forms/evm` authors the form interface, the forms identifier, the declaration, and the mock, with no settings module, and the declaration lookup, no-settings selection admission, and composition admission follow it and precede `chain-forms/factory`; the factory constructs the forms concrete the admitted selection names over the borrowed encoder it takes in its deps, the composition admission comparing the suite-required forms and encoding identifiers as typed values — the encoder's declaration carrying the encoding identifier the forms declaration requires — and returning the family's `Incompatible` flavor before it constructs forms or touches a side effect.
- `chain-forms/evm` holds the EVM suite's identity, entitlement, interval, and chain-identifier forms over the kinds the suite's schemas fix, with address derivation over a public key's canonical bytes and the signature-scheme identifier the signature family owns and exports through its `provides`, and the EIP-712 signed-intent form, constructed over the borrowed encoder it reads its typed-data member types from and declaring the encoding identifier it requires, depending on no signature crate.
- The chain family's factory owns the chain interface, `ISettlementAdapter`, `IEntitlementStateAdapter`, the member identifier, and the declaration and settings, with the settlement and entitlement state, the attempt context, and the family's description of a transaction's estimated fee; the family-owned functions are the quorum view, the declaration lookup `chain/lookup_chain_declaration`, the selection admission `chain/admit_chain_selection`, and the composition admission `chain/admit_chain_composition`, preceding `chain/factory`.
- `chain/base` holds the client, Base's tier mapping, the entitlement-state views, and the events reader, its declaration binding the forms identifier it requires, binding the resolved forms and authoring none, and authors and binds the registry's and the entitlement interface's ABI descriptions.
- `chain/base/settings` owns its settings, its settings-document type and guard, and the admission of its own settings, its declaration stating the endpoint, the gas price oracle, the chain identifier, the entitlement contract, and the precompile encodings, each fixed by the chain or settable within declared bounds with a default, the gas price oracle reaching no family surface.
- The family's declaration lookup, selection admission, and composition admission are each authored once after `chain/base`: the lookup reads the member's declaration without constructing it, the selection admission narrows the untrusted settings document through the member's guard and invokes its settings admission, and the composition admission compares, as typed identifiers, the forms identifier the member's declaration requires with the resolved forms declaration, returning the family's `Incompatible` flavor naming the identifiers that disagree, the member's precompile requirements and provisions compared by the pairing family's composition admission; `chain/factory` follows them, calls the declaration lookup and the composition admission before it constructs anything, and constructs the `base` member the settings-carrying selection names.
- `chain/base` authors the estimated fee of a transaction, from the request its caller hands it and never settled, as the family's description.
- The harness consumes the chain's network values through the family's declaration lookup, selection admission, and composition admission surfaces, constructing through `chain/factory` only after admission.

## Settings service and catalogue

Every value a user may reasonably want to change is a setting: declared once in a catalogue, stored in the configuration registry with its default, its user override, and its scope preserved as typed identifiers, admitted through the owning family's settings admission and, where it touches an adapter, the composition resolver's composition admission before it takes effect, backed up before it changes and restorable exactly, and exposed identically through the CLI, the desktop application, the extension, and the IPC. The installer binary hands the installation coordinator the catalogue's defaults as params, and the coordinator offers the path and consent items during installation. The MVP ships few user-facing settings and the full mechanism, because retrofitting a settings function into a system that hard-coded its values is expensive; the catalogue is authored once, in the daemon composition, after every module whose settings it carries, and every module takes any settings it has as params from the composition root, with no settings supplied for chain forms. This is the pattern mature torrent clients use, and it is the pattern the tables below assume.

**Rules that apply to every setting.**

- **Nothing protocol-fixed is a setting.** A value the deployment's hash-card fixes, the piece-group size, the IV, `τ_soft`, `τ_wallet`, `minSettlementTier`, the suite, is read from the record as the identifier the deployment declares and never from configuration. A client may apply a *stricter* local policy where the specification says a limit is conforming-client policy, never a looser one.
- **Scope is declared.** The catalogue owns the setting-scope identifier, one canonical spelling per member: machine, identity, or project. Machine settings need local presence and a control principal; identity settings follow the identity across its admitted devices; project settings live with the package-manager redirect scope.
- **Presence-required settings are marked.** A setting that names a chain, relayer, discovery, or wallet endpoint, the custody member, the redirect scope or the package managers redirected, the upstream registry URL, update verification, or the request and prefetch consent items requires local presence whatever its scope, and the catalogue marks it, each adapter and endpoint selection carried as its owning family's member or value identifier.
- **Paths are repointable.** Every store root is a setting with a platform default; changing one is a durable job that migrates contents, is checkpointed and reversible, and refuses to point two stores at one location. Nothing in this document is a fixed path; every path shown is the default.
- **Validation before effect.** A change is validated against the catalogue's constraints and, where it touches an adapter, admitted through the owning family's settings admission and the composition resolver's composition admission before it takes effect; an invalid value or an incompatible composition is refused as a typed error carrying the reason, with no side effect.
- **Restart requirements are declared.** A setting states its apply mode — live, next job, or after daemon restart — as the catalogue's apply-mode identifier with one canonical spelling per member, and the surface says so.
- **Secrets are never settings.** A setting may reference custody; it never holds a key, a seed, or a credential.
- **Export and import** produce a versioned document without secrets, for backup and for a second machine, subject to the same validation on import, the identifiers rendered to their canonical spellings only at that document boundary.
- **Every change is traced**: surface, principal, old and new value preserved as their typed identifiers in the consent trace and diagnostics and rendered to canonical spellings only at the output boundary, so a rerouted toolchain is always explainable.

**Catalogue, by category.** Columns: setting, default, scope, applies, MVP exposure. Scope, applies, and exposure are the catalogue's own closed-set identifiers, each with one canonical spelling per member; adapter, endpoint, holding-policy, network-profile, device-role, wallet-provider, telemetry-level, and consent-item values are carried as their owning families' member or value identifiers, the table rendering their canonical spellings. "Exposed" means visible and editable in the MVP surfaces; "mechanism only" means declared and stored but shown only under advanced or diagnostics, so it exists to be exposed later.

# APIs

Five API surfaces exist. The specification fixes the adapter interface signatures, reproduced in the system architecture's Interfaces section; this section covers the surfaces that cross a process or network boundary.

**Package host, npm registry protocol, served by the daemon on a loopback port.** The request's package name is admitted into the package-host family's namespace identifier at the request boundary, unknown text refused by a typed error carrying it, and the identifier preserved through the alias records the response is assembled from. `GET /{package}` returns the packument assembled from the npm adapter's alias records on the ledger, the decrypted tarballs' own `package.json`, and each entry's signed statement, with no local metadata store and no capture at upstream resolution; every ingested version the client can decrypt is listed, so the packument is complete with upstream unavailable, and dist-tags, the source's mutable state, are served best effort. `dist.tarball` URLs are the canonical registry host's, so a committed lockfile carries no machine-specific port and stays portable; npm's registry-host replacement maps them to the local host, where `GET /{package}/-/{name}-{version}.tgz` returns the exact upstream bytes from the CAS after resolution. `GET /-/ping` for readiness. No write endpoints; publishing goes through the CLI, not the registry protocol. Responses carry the upstream integrity value so npm's own verification passes unchanged, and the daemon has already verified the bytes against the attested digest before serving them. During an outage every ingested version the client can decrypt resolves from ledger and swarm alone, ranges included, each version selected from the alias list by npm's semver rules and its dependencies read from its decrypted tarball; dist-tags are best effort and say so (PR-09).

**Daemon IPC, authenticated, local-only.** A control principal is the installing user, established by peer credentials where the platform supplies them or by the per-installation token file where it does not, carried as the `ipc` family's principal-capability identifier; a package caller is anyone who can reach the loopback port. Request classes, each a member of the request-class identifier `apps/daemon` owns with one canonical spelling per class, and what each requires, the presence requirement carried as the `ipc` family's presence-capability identifier: `package.resolve`, `package.status`, any caller;
`status.health`, `status.jobs`, `status.archive`, `status.footprint`, `status.independence` returning per-asset independent or pending with its typed reason identifier and per-project independence, `status.diagnostics`, `settings.schema` returning the catalogue with defaults and its scope, apply-mode, and exposure identifiers, constraints, restart requirements, and presence marks, `settings.get`, `settings.export` without secrets, and `job.repair`, a control principal;
`settings.set` and `settings.reset` on a setting the catalogue does not mark presence-required, a control principal;
`settings.set` and `settings.reset` on a machine-scoped or presence-required setting, `settings.import` with validation and the migration job for path changes, `job.cancel`, `identity.create`, `identity.import`, `identity.registerEnvelopeKeys`, `identity.connectWallet`, `identity.export`, `identity.upgradeCustody`, `device.pair`, `device.admit`, `device.rescope`, `device.revoke`, `entitlement.acquire`, `entitlement.transfer`, `publish.package`, `claim.submit`, and `admin.uninstall`, a control principal with local presence.
Every request carries a correlation identifier and an idempotency key where it mutates.
Framing is length-prefixed messages under the encoding family's adapter, the same encoding used for anything hashed or signed, over the `ipc` family's Unix domain socket concrete on macOS and Linux and its named pipe concrete on Windows, at a path under the configuration directory that is a machine-scoped setting.
Authentication is by peer credentials where the platform supplies them, the connecting process's user identity read from the socket or pipe; where peer credentials are unavailable, a per-installation token file readable only by the installing user is presented on connect. Either mechanism distinguishes the installing user from other local users and nothing finer: every process of the installing user, the CLI, the desktop application, an editor, and an npm lifecycle script alike, is one principal to the operating system, and the daemon does not pretend otherwise. The threat model is therefore same-user. The package host is the package surface and confers resolution and serving only, on any caller. IPC requests from the installing user may read status and the catalogue; every request this table lists under local presence, transfer, key export, identity and binding changes, machine-scoped and presence-required settings, job cancellation, publish, claim, and uninstall, completes only after an interactive confirmation through a surface the daemon owns, the desktop application's dialog or the platform's native prompt, which the CLI invokes rather than replaces: the daemon issues a nonce for the pending request, shows the operation, and applies it only when the confirmation returns that nonce, so no IPC message, script, or automation completes it alone. A principal field in a request is descriptive and confers nothing. The consent trace records the surface, the principal, and the confirmation for every mutating call.

The `ipc` family narrows every untrusted request document through its guard before dispatch, and every mutating or sponsored call completes the required family composition admission — custody, identity, chain, submission, or claim-verifier, as the request's declarations require — before its side effect, a sponsorship denial or exhaustion refused with its typed failure identifier and a claim's proof form carried as the publisher-proof family's proof-class identifier. The request classes and their routing belong to the daemon's composition root, `apps/daemon`, authored after every module they reach; the `ipc` family's server dispatches through a handler interface it owns and names no request class, local routing keyed by the request-class identifier.

**Contract suite, the EVM implementation on Base, the chain family's launch-network member, in the source language the suite declares as the generate family's source-language identifier, Solidity in the MVP; its boundary with the client is its ABI.** Every suite, attestation-source, commitment-scheme, payment-asset, delivery-statement-version and verifier-form, signature-scheme, device-role, request-state, and settlement-tier value below is a provider-owned identifier — the suite in `domain`, the attestation source in the ingest family, the commitment scheme in the hashing family, the payment asset in the payment family, the delivery version in the proof family and the verifier form in the pairing family, the signature scheme in the signature family, the device role in the custody family, and the settlement tier in the chain family — rendered on chain only by its declared wire code, and every adapter-registry and account binding is resolved through the providing family's declaration lookup and composition admission before constructor injection, registration, mutation, voucher, or payment effect.
Registry: `registerAsset`, recording the asset's root and the asserting adapter's alias entry, the ingest-source identifier's signed statement or its recorded absence, appended to the alias key's append-only list and read by alias reads, no entry rebound or removed;
`registerDeployment` with the sidecar-per-live-set check, verifying a declared release attestation's signature on chain through the `IAttestationVerifier` adapter the adapter registry binds per the declared attestation-source identifier, the npm verifier checking P-256 through the precompile at `0x100` against the `sourceKeys` table, admitting a declared absence with the absence recorded, and admitting further escrow deployments of an existing asset from later First Finders;
`addSidecar(deploymentId, parameterSetId, root, locator)` by the asset's authority for a set made live after the deployment's registration; `registerParameterSet`, `retireParameterSet`, `rotateSourceKey` and `revokeSourceKey` under the governance key, `resolve` and `resolveBatch` views.
Envelope keys: `registerEnvelopeKeys(suiteId, keys, proofs, walletSig)`, taking the suite-tagged key material and its proofs of possession as bytes and rejecting identity elements through the bound verifier.
Entitlements: `mint(entitlement, recipient, envelope, mintProof)`, `lock(entitlement, seller, price, paymentAsset, expiry)`, taking the payment family's payment-asset identifier beside the price and moving payment only through the `IPaymentAsset` adapter the adapter registry binds for it,
`deliver(entitlement, previousEnvelope, newEnvelope, transferProof)` which advances the interval, records keys and digest, emits the envelope, transfers, and releases payment in the asset the lock's payment-asset identifier names through that adapter, `withdrawLapsed(lock)`,
`grant(sourceEntitlement, entitlement, recipient, authorEnvelope, envelope, transferProof)` under the escrow suite identifier, checking the author's envelope against the source entitlement's stored digest and closing the recipient's open request for the asset;
`replace(entitlement, envelope, mintProof)` by the issuing authority under a live set, superseding the holder's credential and advancing the interval.
Requests: `requestGrants(assets[])` recording one open request per asset for the signing identity, batched, each request's open or withdrawn disposition carried as a typed request-state identifier; `withdrawRequests(assets[])`; `openRequests(asset, page)` view for holders; an open request authorizes nothing.
The entitlement token, the ERC-721 member behind `IEntitlement` that the adapter registry binds per the entitlement-token-form identifier, overrides its ownership update so that ownership changes only inside `mint`, `grant`, and `deliver`; `transferFrom`, `safeTransferFrom`, `approve`, and `setApprovalForAll` revert; receiver callbacks run after state is final under a reentrancy guard (LC-11).
Authorization: `evaluateAuthorization(context)` and `evaluateAuthorizationBatch(contexts, page)` views returning the three-state result as its typed authorization identifier.
Escrow and claims: `registerEscrow`, `submitClaim(claimSet, voucher)`, `transferAuthority`, `rotateVerifierKey(scheme, keyCommitment)` with the key itself in calldata and the scheme carried as the signature family's scheme identifier, and `revokeVerifierKey`; a voucher is verified through the signature adapter the adapter registry binds for the registered key's signature-scheme identifier.
Identity: the identity is an account whose signer set is the device registry, a contract account implementing ERC-1271 on the EVM suite, created at binding, its signers keyed by scheme-tagged key identifiers and verified through the signature adapter the adapter registry binds for each signature-scheme identifier;
`bindHandshakeKey(scheme, keyCommitment, anchorHash, walletSig)`, the key itself in calldata and the DID Document;

Delivery proofs are checked only through the delivery verifier the adapter registry binds per delivery-statement-version and verifier-form identifier, and payment moves only through the payment-asset adapter it binds per payment-asset identifier, each binding admitted through the providing family's declaration lookup and composition admission before construction or binding. Every proof or voucher is bound to chain, contract, and expiry.
Exact ABI is authored at the contract nodes, the registry's and the entitlement interface's at `chain/base`, which binds them, and the delivery verifier's at `harness-crypto/verifier/evm`, which binds it.

**Relayer service.** `POST /sponsor/binding`, `POST /sponsor/device` for device admission, re-scoping, and revocation, `POST /sponsor/requests` for one batched request per install, and `POST /sponsor/mint` accepting a signed intent or user operation, as the sponsorship mechanism chosen at the relayer milestone dictates, for the one binding, for a batch of requests, and for a free mint or fulfilling grant, applying admission policy in this order, the global per-window expenditure budget and the maximum sponsored liability, then per-identity rate limits, and returning the sponsored transaction hash or an explicit failure state that names exhaustion or denial; `GET /cost` reporting gas spent by category; `GET /budget` reporting the window's remaining budget and outstanding liability. Every sponsored operation reaches the chain through the relayer's `bundler` factory, whose concretes are one per provider, so the provider is a configuration choice. No endpoint sponsors a paid acquisition, whatever payment asset its lock names, and no flood of fresh identities can spend beyond the configured budget (LC-10).

**Claim verifier service.** `POST /claim/verify` accepting a claimant address, a package or claim set, and the proof inputs, running provenance-then-OAuth verification, and returning a voucher over the committed claim set, claimant, contract, chain, nonce, and expiry, signed under a custody-held key of a declared scheme whose scheme identifier and key commitment are registered on chain. `GET /key` returning the current attestation key and its scheme identifier.

**Harness run output.** Every invocation of the harness binary writes the run's outcome, a success carrying every pair's benchmark outcome and every cell's outcome once in iteration order — each outcome held internally as a typed run, pair, cell, completion, incompatibility, or refusal identifier — each naming its members by their typed member and curve identifiers: the files written, the measurements, and the report where the cell ran to completion, its refusal where it refused — and when any pair or cell is refused the error arm carries every pair's and every cell's outcome, rendering only canonical spellings or declared wire codes to each output destination the configuration names as a typed destination identifier: standard output, a file at a configured path, or both, in the configured order, the shipped default being standard output and either overridable from a configuration file or a `--set` flag as every other value is; no `Debug` or `Display` output becomes a key, a measurement, or a document value. A configuration refusal precedes any admitted destination and is written to standard error. The process exits with a success status only when the run succeeds — the run succeeding where every pair and cell is completed or incompatible — and every destination is written. The run, authored after the report, returns the report's document in its outcome.

**Harness report.** A release-evidence JSON document per completed cell, naming the cell's members by their typed identifiers rendered to canonical spellings or declared wire codes at this boundary: sizes of capsule, envelope, and proof; decapsulation time per group; proof generation and verification time; delivery cost for a mint and a transfer through the verifier family's cost description, which carries the chain family's estimated-fee description; the piece-group size and the curve-identifier defaults in force and any adjustment the measurements support.

# Database Schemas

**On-chain state.** Fields per record; types are the MVP suite's Solidity types. Every `Id`, scheme, scope, mode, tier, form, source, role, and request-state field below carries its provider-owned identifier internally with a total mapping to the shown Solidity wire code, no shared identifier type duplicated in `domain` or in the contracts, and every registry and suite composition is admitted through the providing families' declaration lookup and composition admission before any record is written. Suite-specific public material, parameter sets, envelope keys, identity elements, and envelopes, is held as bytes tagged with the suite identifier, with a digest wherever the record binds it, and interpreted only by the delivery verifier the adapter registry binds, so a successor suite changes neither the storage nor the interface; width-bearing fields carry their length or are commitments.

- `SourceKey`: `sourceId uint8` (the ingest family's source identifier), `keyId bytes32`, `key bytes`, `activeFrom uint64`, `revokedAt uint64`; the npm registry's signing keys, updated under the governance key.
- `Escrow`: keyed by `deploymentId bytes32`; `asset bytes32`, `provenance bytes`, `attestation bytes`, `parameterSet bytes32`; one per escrow deployment, so an asset may carry several; no maintainer commitment.
- `Binding`: `identity address`, `handshakeKeyCommitment bytes32` (a commitment to the handshake public key, which travels in the binding transaction's calldata and in the anchored DID Document), `scheme uint8` (the signature family's scheme identifier), `anchorHash bytes32`.
- `IntentNonce`: key `identity address`; value `uint64`, advanced on every accepted signed intent.
- `DeviceSigner`: keyed by `identity address` and `keyId bytes32` (a scheme-tagged key identifier, whose signatures the account verifies through the signature adapter the adapter registry binds for that signature-scheme identifier); `scheme uint8` (the signature family's scheme identifier), `role uint8` (the custody family's device-role identifier), `permissions uint256`, `admittedAt uint64`, `revokedAt uint64`; the identity account's signer set, a contract account on the EVM suite, read by `isDeviceAdmitted`.
- `Request`: keyed by `identity address` and `asset bytes32`; `openedAt uint64`, `closedAt uint64`, `closedBy` grant or withdrawal (the request's closed disposition carried as its typed request-state identifier); submitted in batches; readable per asset by holders.
- `VerifierKey`: `scheme uint8` (the signature family's scheme identifier, whose signature adapter the adapter registry binds), `keyCommitment bytes32` (a commitment to the attestation public key, which travels in the calldata of the registering or rotating transaction), `activeFrom uint64`, `revokedAt uint64`.
- Events carry the full envelope and proof at every mint, grant, and transfer.

**Daemon local stores, tables of the storage-engine family, `storage/redb` in the MVP.** Keys and values under the encoding family's adapter; no consumer names the store library. Every closed-set field below is preserved as its owning identifier type — job kind and state, resolution path and outcome, holding reason, request state and pending reason, health and view outcome, settings scope and apply mode, consent operation, and metric category — as storage keys and values, never text, and rendered to canonical spellings only in exports and diagnostics.

- `jobs`: key job id; value kind and state as their typed job identifiers, checkpoint, idempotency key, created, updated, error.
- `resolutions`: key request correlation id; value path chosen and outcome as their typed resolution identifiers, timings.
- `ciphertext_index`: key ciphertext root; value size, transports and locators as the transport family's member and locator identifiers, holding reason as the seed-host family's holding-kind identifier — obligated or voluntary, sidecar roots held.
- `cas_index`: key content hash; value size, upstream integrity, resolving projects, pinned, last used.
- `entitlements`: key entitlement id; value asset, parameter set, interval index, envelope digest, custody reference to the persistent credential; never the decrypted credential.
- `requests`: key asset; value state as its typed request-state identifier — queued, submitted, open, fulfilled, withdrawn, or refused — with the batch's transaction reference, the reason as its typed pending-reason identifier when pending, and timestamps.
- `independence`: key asset; value plaintext held, ciphertext held with its parameter sets, credential held with its set, independent or pending as its typed independence-state identifier with its reason identifier, and the projects whose lockfiles name the asset.
- `state_views`: key node id and reference; value tier as the chain family's settlement-tier identifier, timestamp, result cache bounded by `τ_soft`.
- `settings`: key catalogue path; value schema version, default, user override, scope and restart requirement as the catalogue's scope and apply-mode identifiers, constraint reference, last changed by surface and principal; secrets only as custody references.
- `health`: key subsystem; value state as its typed health identifier and last probe.
- `consent_trace`: key event id; value surface, principal, and operation as its typed consent-operation identifier, shown-and-acted flags, timestamp.

**Plaintext CAS layout, the `cas/filesystem` concrete.** Under the configured CAS root, whose default is the platform application-data directory's `cas` and which the user repoints at install or in settings: `{cas_root}/{hash[0..2]}/{hash[2..4]}/{hash}/` holding the tarball and a manifest; `{cas_root}/tmp/` for uncommitted writes on the same volume so commit is a rename. Package managers extract from the served tarball into each project; nothing links into the CAS, so reuse is at the tarball and a root change is a migration job that moves entries and updates the index.

**Ciphertext store layout, owned mode.** Under the configured store root, default the platform application-data directory's `swarm`, repointable the same way: `{swarm_root}/{root[0..2]}/{root}/` holding an object's pieces and its Bao outboard; a ciphertext and each of its sidecars are separate objects under their own roots. Under `seed-host/rqbit` the layout is the rqbit application's, whose download directory is itself a setting of that application, and the concrete translates. The two roots may not coincide.

**Custody store, the `custody/local_keystore` concrete.** Under the OS credential store, a wrapping key per identity; on disk, encrypted blobs each headed by the custody member's identifier and its version identifier, the wrapping-key identifier, and the derivation parameters. A root holds the holder seed, from which the control branch and the envelope branch derive; every device holds its own device key, its role as the custody family's device-role identifier, and its delegation from the handshake key; a reader holds the envelope secrets; each holds per-entitlement persistent credentials as envelope plus interval index, publisher seed if any, and master scalars if any. An in-place upgrade writes the new blob beside the old, verifies the re-derived public keys against the on-chain binding and envelope-key registration, and only then retires the old blob and its wrapping key. Never the decrypted credential or a piece-group key; the seed never on a non-root device.

**Metrics schema.** One record per install with correlation id, each resolution path, reason, request and grant outcome, job outcome, health state, and category carried as its owning identifier and rendered to its canonical spelling only at the export boundary: resolution path and its reason, CAS hit, cross-project reuse count, request registered and its batch, grant landed and the time from request, ciphertext held, fraction of the closure independent at the end of the run, wall-clock, authorization fraction, state-read count and latency, decapsulation time per group and group count, swarm bytes, ingest bytes, relayer gas, each settlement's fee through the chain family's fee description, job outcomes, adapter health snapshot. No identifying data beyond what the ledger publishes.

**Sample deployment for the site demonstration.** A generated asset with a small plaintext, its ciphertext and sidecar, a parameter set, and one sample credential, bundled with the WebAssembly build; generated by the `sample_deployment` node of the payload cipher milestone, since it needs the cipher.

# Proposed File Tree

Each function is a module directory with one file per element: `interface.rs`, `interaction.spec.md`, `mock.rs`, `test.rs`, `mod.rs` for the implementation, and `provides.rs` for the public surface; test files attach as `#[cfg(test)]` modules and `mock.rs` sits behind a `mocks` feature. An adapter is one struct in one file implementing the repo-owned trait, so a concrete adapter's operations are its methods and claim no files of their own. An interface lives in the module it provides an interface for and is authored in the node of the first consumer that needs it.

The layout is general responsibility, then functional need, then concrete implementation. Every adapter family is a crate under `adapters/`, whose `factory` module is the family's factory: its `interface.rs` declares the generic trait, with a type only a concrete produces named as an associated type of the trait, the member identifier, each value identifier the family provides, and the capability declaration every concrete fills in carrying its member's identifier rather than a duplicate text name, and the factory function's signature and types; its `mod.rs` holds the factory function; its `provides.rs` is the crate's public surface. The crate root `lib.rs` is a barrel that declares each concrete as a private module and each function the family owns as a module — the concrete's `crate/concrete/settings`, the family's `crate/lookup_<family>_declaration`, `crate/admit_<family>_selection`, and `crate/admit_<family>_composition` — re-exports the factory module's provides and every family-owned function's provides, and holds nothing else; each module's node adds its own line there as its provides step. A concrete is a directory under `src/` named by its explicit noun, holding the same elements with `mod.rs` holding the adapter that implements the generic trait and a `provides.rs` visible to the crate only; a function a concrete owns, its settings-document guard and settings admission among them, is a module directory beneath the concrete.
The factory module's interface and mock are authored in the node of the family's first ticket, its first concrete or a family-owned function preceding it, the first source file that requires them, together with that node's own implementation, and that node creates the crate; each further concrete and family-owned function is its own node; each concrete's settings admission, where it has one, precedes that concrete, and the family's declaration lookup, selection admission, and composition admission follow the concretes and the settings admissions they compose.

Within a milestone, the factory module's implementation, test, and provides are authored once in the factory node, after the family's last concrete in that milestone and its admission functions, since the factory function calls the composition admission before it constructs anything, and before every consumer in that milestone. Adding a concrete in a later milestone reopens and edits the factory and each applicable family location through that concrete's complete increment — its settings increment where it has a settable value, with no settings increment where it has none — with the factory branch following the concrete's settings and admission increments.
A consumer depends on the family crate and sees the factory's surface and nothing beneath it, and its node follows whichever admission surface it uses — the selection admission for an admitted selection, the declaration lookup for a declaration, the composition admission for an admitted composition, or the factory for a constructed member — and never a concrete or the factory alone where an admission surface supplies the value.
The factory function takes the admitted member identifier, from configuration or from a deployment's hash-card, and the selection the selection admission returns, carrying admitted settings only where the concrete has settable values and refusing supplied settings otherwise, calls the composition admission to compare the member's declared requirements against the declarations of the upstream members it composes with, and returns the constructed adapter behind the generic trait or refuses with the family's `Incompatible` before it constructs anything or any side effect begins. A family exists even where the MVP ships one concrete.

A Solidity contract is a module of `contracts/evm/src/`, the EVM suite that is the on-chain concrete of the chain family's Base concrete, with its Foundry tests as its interface, guard, and unit elements, its constants and vector record types generated from the Rust reference, and its test vectors read from the committed vector files the Rust reference generates; a further declared chain-forms member provides a further suite under `contracts/<suite>` with its own toolchain. Crate directories are hyphenated and module directories underscored. A family's factory node is `crate/factory`, a concrete's `crate/concrete`, a function a concrete owns `crate/concrete/function`, a function the family owns `crate/function`, a module of the domain or workflows crate `crate/module`, and a contract `contracts/<suite>/Contract`. The tree is the shape the workspace reaches; each crate is created by the node of the first module that lives in it, which for a family is its first ticket, a concrete or a family-owned function preceding it, and the workspace manifest's glob members, `crates/*`, `adapters/*`, and `apps/*`, admit it without an edit, which is why every direct child of those directories is a crate and nothing nests a crate directory inside another. A configuration file is authored once, complete, by the node that creates it; a crate's manifest and barrel accumulate one entry per module from that module's node.
